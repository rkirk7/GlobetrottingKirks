Safaris


    Whenever we're asked our favorite type of travel, it's hard to come up with an answer. However, we've gone on safari more times than we've done anything else--which should tell you how much we love the experience. We specialize in safari trips and would love to help you plan your dream safari.

    Botswana

    We've been to Botswana twice. The Okavango Delta is such a special place; if you've seen a nature documentary about wildlife in Africa, there's a very good chance you saw a video of the delta! We've been privileged to have stunning animal encounters here, including a rare sighting of two cheetahs swimming across a river. Botswana is also the only place we've visited on safari where we had a female guide, part of an inspiring program to encourage more women to become guides!

    What makes Botswana stand out:

    • The rich diversity of life in the Okavango Delta
    • When the floodwaters come in, the animals congregate in smaller spaces, making them easier to see
    • Small camps with an intimate atmosphere

    South Africa

    South Africa is famous for Kruger National Park. Attached to the park are private reserves which house lodges that have more flexibility than those located in the park proper. Sabi Sands is one such reserve, and we had an absolutely amazing time there! This might be the best place we've visited in terms of leopard sightings and the likelihood of seeing the Big 5 (lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo, rhino) in one park. In fact, if you're lucky, you might see them in one drive!

    What makes South Africa (and specifically Sabi Sands) stand out:

    • Good chance of seeing all of the Big 5
    • Numerous leopards
    • A massive pride of lions. We've never seen so many in one place.
    • Also, a couple very rare albino lions.
    • A lot of wildlife in a relatively small place makes for great game viewing
    • More convenient than a lot of places since you stay within South Africa (assuming you fly into Johannesberg or Cape Town).

    Zimbabwe

    Hwange National Park is the largest in the country and is known as the home for about 50,000 elephants. As a result, they gather around the sparse watering holes and are a delight to watch. Somalisa Acacia, located within the park, is the only lodge we've ever returned to--and we've been three times! That should tell you how much we love it. There is nothing to match the thrill of sitting on your deck and watching the elephants frolic right in front of you. In addition to the elephants, there are lions, leopards, buffalo, antelopes of all sorts, and a lot of interesting bird life.

    What makes Zimbabwe stand out:

    • So many elephants. Just incredible.
    • Witness famous Victoria Falls.
    • A well-established resident pride of lions with a long history. Cecil, the lion famously killed illegally by a dentist, used to be the leader of the pride.
    • Good likelihood of seeing leopards.
    • Opportunities to do approach walks. This was an amazing experience.

    Zambia

    We've visited Zambia twice, and on both occasions we've had excellent sightings and experiences. Here, you can watch elephants cross a river, listen to the hippos honking outside your tent at night, and find lions and wild dogs lounging or hunting.

    What makes Zambia stand out:

    • Two rivers (the Zambezi and South Luangwa) serve as strong attractors for a lot of wildlife
    • Fishing and other water activities, such as canoeing
    • Arrive at some camps by boat, which is an adventure, especially at night

    Tanzania

    Tanzania was our first safari, and we recommend it as a first safari for many people. Here, you can witness the sweeping plains and endless hordes of wildebeest and other migrating animals. Take a hot air balloon ride over the Serengeti at sunrise as hyenas chase ostriches below you--there's nothing like it.

    What makes Tanzania stand out:

    • The Serengeti is famous for a reason. This is what you think of when you imagine the African savannah.
    • The Great Migration passes through, creating an astonishing spectacle of millions of traveling wildebeest, zebras, elephants, and other animals--with predators close on their heels.
    • River crossings: these can be seen in Tanzania and Kenya. We tried to see them in Kenya and never quite managed it.
    • Incredible sunrise hot air balloon experiences.
    • Ngorongoro Crater is an oasis; the animals within remain in the crater year-round, creating a fascinating microcosm of life.
    • You can see all of the Big 5.
    • One of the only places to find the rare tree-climbing lions!

    Kenya

    The Masai Mara is a special place; river crossings; Great Migration; saw the big five in one drive as well as two kills.

    What makes Kenya stand out:

    • River crossings: This is the other place where you can witness the Great Migration crossing the river, an amazing spectacle. We still haven't witnessed it, ourselves
    • Fantastic variety of wildlife; we saw the Big 5 on one drive.
    • Some camps use Maasai as guides.
    • Like Tanzania, the hot air balloon ride here is not to be missed!
    • By luck or our guides' skill, the only successful kills we've seen in Africa have been in the Masai Mara (two cheetah kills...and one horrifying babboon kill of a small antelope). We've seen many unsuccessful lion hunts.

    Namibia

    Namibia isn't as popular of a safari destination, but it has a lot to offer. Here, you can visit a reserve where pangolins are protected--and, if you're lucky, see one for yourself! Namibia is home to many rhinos and leopards. Beyond our typical animal experiences, Namibia also possesses Sossusvlei, the oldest desert in the world, with glorious, sweeping sand dunes. You can also visit Walvis Bay for the astonishing views and masses of happy seals.

    What makes Namibia stand out:

    • Sossusvlei, the oldest desert in the world. Hike the Big Daddy sand dune and take a helicopter ride to see it from above.
    • Walvis Bay, home to thousands of playful seals.
    • Visit a pangolin reserve and go on a special nighttime excursion to see one in the wild!
    • Good diversity of wildlife; we had great night sightings of animals at the watering hole of one of our lodges
    • You can drive yourself around much of the country...although flat tires are likely!
