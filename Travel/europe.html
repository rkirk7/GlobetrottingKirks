Europe


Europe is the second smallest continent but boasts some 50 countries (depending on who you ask). We've been to most of Europe multiple times, via cruises, self-drive and tours--but there’s so much more to discover!

I am interested in going to Europe!

    Italy

    Everyone's favorite these days, and for good reason: Italy is packed with a remarkable variety of landscapes, history, art, culture and food. It takes multiple visits to begin to fully appreciate a dolce vita—the sweet life of Italy. Head to the three crown jewels: Venice, Florence and Rome; add on Amalfi or Cinque Terre; wander to islands like Sicily, Sardina, Capri or Ischia; or go off the beaten path to the Puglia region.

    France

    Really, you could just immerse in spectacular Paris. France boasts amazing historical sites, fabulous landscapes from mountains to beaches, and world-class food, fashion and art. From Paris, go north to check out Normandy and Mont St. Michel; over to the Loire valley or Burgundy for vineyards, south to sweet Provence, and down to glitzy Cote d’Azur with Nice, Cannes and Monaco.

    Greece

    Known for its stunning sea views and ancient history, it’s a popular cruise destination with stops at most major sites. But we really recommend spending time on individual islands to get the most out of Greece. We love Santorini (after the day trippers are gone) and Naxos; and exploring northern Greece’s ruins at Delphi, and the astonishing perched monasteries at Meteora.

    Highlights include:

    • The islands, especially Santorini and Naxos
    • The history. Explore the ruins at Delphi and the ancient agora and Parthenon in Athens.
    • The food and culture!

    Ireland

    This small island is packed with history, castles galore and coastal beauty. You can get a fast-paced taste in a week, but better to take 10 days. Enjoy lively Dublin, go southwest toward the rings of Kerry and Derry, up to the Cliffs of Moher and Galway before returning to Dublin. With more time, check out Northern Ireland’s beauty and remnants of “the troubles.”

    Switzerland

    Sure, there are nice cities here but it’s the pristine lakes and spectacular mountains that define this small country. Highlights include glitzy St. Moritz, the Matterhorn in Zermatt, famed Lake Geneva, and pretty mountain-ringed Lake Lucerne. The crown jewels are in the Berner Oberland region, featuring Lauterbrunnen in the valley, and Wengen, Murren and Grindelwald in the mountains, with access to the “top of Europe” at Jungfrau. You don’t even need a car, thanks to remarkably efficient and scenic trains and cable cars.

    Iceland

    From bleak lava fields to lush meadows, vast glaciers to shapely craters to gushing waterfalls: Iceland combines the best of Hawaii, New Zealand, Ireland and Scotland! Cruises will stop at major ports, but a self-drive is the best way to soak in all of Iceland. From Reykejvik, you can start with the Golden Circle, circle clockwise to the charming Snaefellsnes Peninsula, up to Husavik for the best whale watching and nearby Dettifoss—the BEST waterfall, around to Jokulsarlon Glacier Lagoon, the crown jewel of Iceland.

    The UK: England, Scotland, Wales, and North Ireland

    You can spend weeks in each locale of the UK, or attempt to circle Scotland, England and Wales in one long trip (we took a month). Highlights include Scotland’s Edinburgh and Isle of Sky; Wales’ Caernarfon Castle (the seat of the Prince of Wales) sweet Conwy town and Snowdonia National Park (where Sir Edmund Hillary trained); and England’s world-class London, Windsor and many other famed castles, charming York, Stonehenge, white cliffs and much more. Our favorite part of England: gorgeous Cornwall.

    Croatia

    Featuring rugged mountains, historic walled towns and numerous islands, Croatia has soared in popularity. Many large cruise ships are making a few stops while smaller yachts meander the Dalmatian Coast from Split to Dubrovnik, stopping at darling isles such as Hvar, Korcula and, our favorite, Rovinj. While in the neighborhood, add time to check out Slovenia, Bosnia & Herzegovina, or Montenegro.

    Spain

    This large, fascinating country has more UNESCO World Heritage Sites than any other, including the Moorish Alhambra, charming towns of Toledo and Cordoba, aqueduct of Segovia; two national parks in the Canary islands, and, of course, Gaudi’s Sagrada Familia in Barcelona. Mountains, beaches, countryside: you name it, Spain has it.

    Portugal

    Looking for an authentic, gentler slice of Europe? Head for Portugal, with its coastline, vineyards, rugged mountains, and charming hills. Highlights include Lisbon and nearby Sintra, north to Porto and nearby Douro Valley, or south to the Algarve coast and charming Tavira. Add enough time to fly out to the Azores (comprised of nine islands) for volcanic crater lakes and hot springs, and Madeira for lush forests, high cliffs and levada hikes through historic irrigation channels. Around June-August, you can catch whales at both island groups.

    Norway

    Known for dramatic fjords and mountains, Viking history and the Northern Lights, Norway draws nature lovers and adventurers. Many cruise ships stop at key cities including Oslo, Bergen and Stavanger, where you can hike up Pulpit Rock. Take the gorgeous Flåm railway (part of the Bergen-Oslo line) then a fjord cruise on Nærøyfjord or Aurlandsfjord. At the northern end of Norway lie Tromso, charming Senja and the stunning Lofoten Islands, with peaks and views galore. And at the extreme north is Svalbard, an archipelago just 800 miles from the North Pole that features polar bears, walruses, glaciers, dog sledding and more. Note: The best times to catch the Northern Lights are from September to March; the longest days with the Midnight Sun are May-July.

    Germany

    Known best for castles, history, Oktoberfest and the Black Forest, Germany has something for everyone. Visit Berlin, Dachau and Nuremberg for sobering history; enjoy cruising along its many rivers include the Rhine, Main, Moselle and Danube; party during Oktoberfest in Munich (note: it usually wraps in early October!); check out charming Christmas markets in Cologne and Dresden, or ski in the Bavarian Alps near pretty Garmisch-Partenkirchen; take drives or trains along the romantic road or Moselle Valley; and, of course, marvel at fairy-tale Neuschwanstein Castle, which inspired Disney’s Cinderella castle—it’s especially gorgeous in the winter!

    The Netherlands

    Famed for its canals, tulips, cycling lifestyle and inclusiveness as a LGBTQ+-friendly country, there are actually 12 provinces in the Netherlands. North and South Holland are the power houses with Amsterdam, Rotterdam and The Hague. Bustling Amsterdam features the vast Van Gogh Museum and the gripping Anne Frank House, along with canal cruises, the red light district and many boisterous events. Tulips usually hit their peak in April, when the Keukenhof Gardens (“the garden of Europe”) are packed, along with the entire bulb region of Bollenstreek. For a less crowded experience, head further north to Noordoostpolder in the Flevoland province, where locals go to appreciate the blooms.
